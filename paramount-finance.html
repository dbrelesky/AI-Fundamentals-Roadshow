What it does to the
businesses we’re in.

Making content trends toward near-zero cost. When supply explodes, the moat moves.

2026 — Now ~2028 — Next
Production & post AI dubbing, localization, captioning, rough cuts and VFX assists at volume; humans finish. Versioning per market and format becomes near-free; the edit and the taste are the value.
Streaming & discovery Sharper recommendations and catalog search; AI-assisted metadata and promos. The home screen assembles per viewer; “ask, don’t browse” is how people find content.
Content & marketing Recaps, social cutdowns, trailers and copy generated at volume; teams curate. Content commoditizes; the edge is POV, real talent, and trusted IP — not volume.
The moat Library, rights, and brand stay scarce while tools commoditize. Value concentrates in rights, distribution, and trust — what AI can’t generate.

Our move: Use AI to lower cost and personalize — lean into IP, talent, rights, and audience trust.

How AI changes what
fans expect.

Behaviors we’re already seeing, and where they head.

What we’re seeing now · 2026

  • Fans ask AI instead of searching, fewer clicks to publishers
  • Summary-first: “just tell me who won and why”
  • Talking to assistants conversationally, second-screen all game
  • Rising “is this even real?” doubt as AI content floods feeds

What may come · ~2028

  • A personal AI companion that knows your teams, fantasy and bets
  • Content comes to you, auto-shaped to your format and length
  • Ask, don’t browse: conversation becomes how fans discover
  • Trust premium: verified sources and real talent win attention

What it means: fans will expect answers, personalization, and proof. Our edge is real access, real talent, and trust no generic bot can fake.
