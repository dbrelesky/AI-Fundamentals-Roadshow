If You Remember One Thing
AI is as big a deal as the internet or mobile —
and only as big.

Evans’ deliberately calibrated take: this is a genuine platform shift that will touch every part of how we work — not a god, not magic, and not the end of work. For finance, that’s the useful frame: plan for it like you’d plan for the internet, not for science fiction.

Where We Are

We’re in the “1997 moment” of AI

Early, exciting, and deeply uncertain about what comes next — just like the web in 1997.

What we know

  • This is real and it’s big — a true platform shift.
  • Adoption is moving fast; the tools genuinely work.
  • Every company will have to engage with it.

What we don’t know yet

  • Which companies win, and which business models stick.
  • Where the durable profit pools end up.
  • How much better the models actually get.
The 1997 lesson: in 1997 we knew the web mattered but couldn’t have named Google or Amazon as the winners. Same here — conviction on the shift, humility on the specifics.
A Lesson Finance Already Lived

Remember what the spreadsheet did

Evans’ favorite analogy — and it’s ours. VisiCalc and Excel automated the core of accounting. Accountants didn’t disappear; the job grew.

  • The grunt work got automated.Recalculating ledgers by hand vanished overnight.
  • The profession got bigger, not smaller.Cheaper analysis meant we did far more of it — modeling, scenarios, FP&A. (Jevons paradox: efficiency drives more demand.)
  • The job moved up to judgment.Less manual calculation, more interpretation, advising and decisions.
AI is the next spreadsheet moment for knowledge work — the tool changes, the people move up the value chain. The finance-team takeaway
Source: Evans on VisiCalc / Excel and the Jevons paradox.
The Reframe That Matters Most

Ask “task or job?” — not “what % can AI do?”

Evans’ sharpest point on work: AI is brilliant at tasks. A job is a bundle of tasks plus judgment, accountability and relationships.

A task

Bounded, repeatable, checkable
  • Draft the variance commentary
  • Summarize the 80-page filing
  • Reformat the data, build the first model

AI does these brilliantly — and a person reviews.

A job

Tasks + judgment + ownership
  • Deciding what the numbers mean
  • Owning the forecast in front of the board
  • Negotiating, advising, being accountable

AI takes tasks off the plate; the job remains human.

Finance read: AI won’t replace your team — it removes tasks so they spend more time on judgment. That’s a capacity story, not a headcount story.
The Surprising Part

AI is driving a boom in consulting

Counterintuitive but real: the AI wave is fuelling a surge in professional services — McKinsey, Bain, Accenture, Fujitsu. Buying the model is easy; deploying it is hard.

  • Technology ≠ adoption.Capability in a lab doesn’t change a business until it’s wired into real workflows, data and controls.
  • The hard part is the last mile.Process redesign, change management, governance and integration — that’s where the money and the time go.
  • Budget the deployment, not just the license.For finance: the line item that matters isn’t the per-seat AI subscription — it’s the people and process work around it.
Source: Evans on the professional-services boom around AI deployment.
Where Value Accrues

Models commoditize. Distribution wins.

As software gets dramatically easier to build, the scarce thing isn’t the model — it’s getting to the customer.

1
The model

Converging fast; increasingly a commodity. Hard to hold a durable edge.

2
The application

Value moves up the stack — products built on top of the models.

3
Distribution

The ultimate moat. Whoever is closest to the customer at the moment of choice wins.

The distribution wars: Google, Meta, Apple and OpenAI are fighting over who owns the customer relationship — not who has the cleverest model.

The Counter-Current

The anti-AI backlash is real

Alongside the boom, a genuine public skepticism and fatigue is building — and it shapes how audiences receive AI-made media.

What’s driving it

  • Job anxiety and “slop” fatigue as AI content floods feeds
  • Distrust of authenticity — “is this even real?”
  • Emotion, not logic: you can’t argue people out of a feeling

Why it matters to media

  • Trust and authenticity become competitive assets
  • Real talent and verified sources earn a premium
  • Brand reputation is exposed if AI is used carelessly
Finance read: the backlash is a risk to model and a reason our trusted IP and talent get more valuable, not less.
Impact On Media · Now & Next

What it does to the businesses we’re in

The cost of making content trends toward near-zero. When supply explodes, the scarce thing — and the moat — moves.

2026 — Happening Now~2028 — Where It Heads
Production & post AI dubbing, localization, captioning, rough cuts and VFX assists at volume; humans finish. Versioning per market and format becomes near-free; the edit and the taste are the value.
Streaming & discovery Sharper recommendations and catalog search; AI-assisted metadata and promos. The home screen assembles itself per viewer; “ask, don’t browse” becomes how people find content.
Content & marketing Recaps, social cutdowns, trailers and copy generated at volume; teams curate. Content gets commoditized; the edge is a distinct POV, real talent and trusted IP — not volume.
The moat Library, rights and brand stay scarce while tools commoditize. Value concentrates in rights, distribution and trust — the things AI can’t generate.
Our move: use AI to lower cost and personalize — while leaning into the assets it can’t fake: IP, talent, rights and audience trust.
Why This Favors Us

AI makes our scarce assets worth more

  • When making content is cheap, owning it is the edge.A flood of generic content makes iconic, owned IP and franchises stand out further.
  • Distribution is the moat — and we have it.Direct audience relationships, platforms and rights are exactly what Evans says wins as models commoditize.
  • Trust and real talent earn a premium.In a backlash era, verified, human, premium content is the antidote to “slop.”
Bottom line: AI lowers the cost of the commodity and raises the value of what only Paramount can offer.

The Takeaway For Paramount

What this means for us

Now · 2026

  • Point AI at tasks: localization, recaps, summaries, first-draft models and memos.
  • Move teams from trial to habit — that’s where the capacity gain is.
  • Budget for deployment and change, not just licenses.
  • Keep humans on anything that must be right, not just fast.

Next · ~2028

  • Content gets cheap; invest in the moat: IP, rights, talent, trust, distribution.
  • Plan for cheaper compute even if the market corrects — the rails stay.
  • Watch the shift from “browse” to “ask” in how audiences discover us.
  • Treat the backlash as a reason to lead on trust and authenticity.
Evans’ reassurance: things will probably be okay — if you stay curious, treat AI as a tool for tasks, and keep building judgment.
