If You Remember One Thing
This is a platform shift, not a feature.
The infrastructure is being built now — but who captures the value is still up for grabs.

Evans’ core point: every 10–15 years a new computing platform resets the industry. We’re in the messy, early, over-invested phase of the fifth one. For Paramount, the question isn’t “is AI real” — it’s where our moat moves to when making content gets cheap.

The Question Everyone Asks

Bubble… or platform shift?

Evans’ answer: it can be both. The investment may overshoot — but the infrastructure outlasts the crash.

The bubble risk is real

  • We’re building over-capacity in GPUs and energy ahead of revenue.
  • If the near-term revenue doesn’t show up, there is a correction.
  • Usage today is still casual; monetization is thin (~5% pay).

…but the rails remain

  • Like railways and fiber: the bubble funds the build-out, the crash wipes investors — the infrastructure stays.
  • What’s left is cheap, abundant compute that fuels the next decade of products.
  • “Abundant cognition” becomes a utility you build on top of.
Finance read: separate the asset from the equity story. Even a market correction leaves cheaper AI for everyone — including us.
The Trust Problem

“Better” is not the same as “right”

Evans’ sharpest point for anyone deploying AI: a lower error rate is not zero error. Design for where that matters.

“Better”

Lower error rate, probabilistic

Great when a strong draft, a summary, or 20 options is the goal and a human edits. The error you can catch is cheap.

“Right”

Deterministic, must-be-correct

There’s a world of difference between a legal brief (or a financial statement) that looks right and one that is right. Here you keep a human in the loop.

Finance read: lean in on drafting, research and analysis acceleration; keep hands on the wheel for numbers, filings, contracts and anything auditable.

Evans’ framing: the story splits into three buckets — Capital (the build-out), Deployment (getting it into work), and Change (what it does to industries).
Impact On Media · Now & Next

What it does to the businesses we’re in

The cost of making content trends toward near-zero. When supply explodes, the scarce thing — and the moat — moves.

2026 — Happening Now~2028 — Where It Heads
Production & post AI dubbing, localization, captioning, rough cuts and VFX assists drafted at volume; humans finish. Versioning per market and format becomes near-free; the edit and the taste are the value.
Streaming & discovery Sharper recommendations and catalog search; AI-assisted metadata and promos. The home screen assembles itself per viewer; “ask, don’t browse” becomes how people find content.
Content & marketing Recaps, social cutdowns, trailers and copy generated at volume; teams curate. Content gets commoditized; the edge is a distinct POV, real talent, and trusted IP — not volume.
The moat Library, rights and brand still scarce while tools commoditize. Value concentrates in rights, distribution and trust — the things AI can’t generate.
Our move: use AI to lower cost and personalize — while leaning into the assets it can’t fake: IP, talent, rights and audience trust.
Where Value Lands

Models commoditize. Distribution wins.

  • The models are converging. Evans’ view: the underlying LLMs increasingly look like commodities — the gap between them keeps narrowing.
  • Brand and distribution decide winners. ChatGPT took the brand slot the way Google did for search. The company closest to the user at the moment of choice wins — not necessarily the best engineering.
  • Why that’s good news for Paramount. We already own scarce, defensible things: iconic IP, live rights, talent relationships and direct audience distribution. AI makes the commodity cheaper; it makes our scarce assets more valuable.
Source: Benedict Evans on model commoditization and distribution.

The Takeaway For Paramount

What this means for us

Now · 2026

  • Treat AI as a cost lever: localization, recaps, marketing, back-office and analysis drafted at volume.
  • Move teams from trial to habit — that’s where the productivity is.
  • Keep humans on anything that must be right, not just better.

Next · ~2028

  • Content gets cheap; defend and invest in the moat: IP, rights, talent, trust, distribution.
  • Plan for cheaper compute even if the market corrects — the rails stay.
  • Watch the shift from “browse” to “ask” in how audiences discover us.
Bottom line: AI makes the commodity cheaper and our scarce assets more valuable. Win by deploying fast where it’s safe — and protecting what only Paramount can offer.
